# Executive Summary

The flow-feature transfer-function thrust extends Aim 2's AI transfer-function work from medical imaging to computational fluid dynamics (CFD). Earlier 2026 discussions noted that transferring a transfer function across simulation data first requires the relevant scientific features to be characterized, much as segmentation does in the MRI example. This work supplies that characterization. A neural network reads non-dimensional velocity and pressure fields and predicts, for every voxel, the probability that it belongs to one of four flow features: recirculation, vortex, shear layer, or wake. A custom VTK GPU shader turns those four probabilities into color and opacity, so a user can see feature-based structure without tuning thresholds on the Q-criterion, the λ₂-criterion, or vorticity magnitude.

Training data comes from AhmedML, a public dataset of 499 high-fidelity, time-averaged simulations of the Ahmed body with slant angles from 6° to 70°. A VTK-based pipeline reads each native unstructured mesh, computes Q\*, λ₂\*, |ω|\*, total-pressure coefficient Cpt, and a reversed-flow flag on that mesh, and resamples inputs and ingredients onto a uniform grid at dx = H/32. Fixed rules then generate the labels. The network sees only the five input channels: u, v, and w normalized by the free-stream velocity, the pressure coefficient Cp, and a fluid mask. The derived quantities used to make the labels are never shown to the model. Runs are split by slant angle and stratified by wake regimes.

A label audit across 24 cases showed that the classes are rare and unbalanced: in a representative case only 0.24% of fluid voxels are vortex, 0.47% recirculation, 0.81% shear layer, and 5.6% wake. The audit also showed sharp changes in recirculation and wake length near the Ahmed body's critical slant angle of about 30–35°, and some thresholds sit close to the tails of their distributions.

In a first baseline run, a compact 3D U-Net with 0.34 million parameters was trained for 3,000 steps on 64³ patches using combined BCE and Dice loss. It reached a validation IoU of about 0.88 for wake, 0.83 for recirculation, 0.56 for vortex, and 0.54 for shear layer, a mean of about 0.70. The thin, gradient-defined classes are the hardest, as expected. The custom four-channel shader was checked against an independent NumPy ray-cast reference and matched within a median error of 1/255. By contrast, VTK's built-in direct-RGBA volume path differed by 102/255 on the same scene, which is why the custom shader is needed.

The architecture question is still open. The next steps are to compare the U-Net against a plain 3D CNN and other variants under one protocol, choose per-class thresholds on validation data, and report IoU, precision, and recall on the held-out test set. Possible follow-on work includes transfer to WindsorML, DrivAerML, and HiLiftAeroML, ONNX export for GPU inference inside ParaView, and body-type-specific presets.
