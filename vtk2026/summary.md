# Executive Summary

VTK's 2026 activity combined four complementary mechanisms: a regular release line, a sustained technical blog series, a focused community hackathon, and planning for a broader VTK Days event  (see [repository](https://gitlab.kitware.com/vtk/vtk) and [website](https://vtk.org/)). VTK 9.6.0 established the year's baseline in February, followed by VTK 9.6.1 in March and VTK 9.6.2 in May. The release line included generated JavaScript wrappers and WebAssembly sessions, native ONNX inference, Wayland and Java improvements, an AMR data-model refactor, Python free-threading support, faster array and observer operations, and extensive filter, rendering, and I/O changes. Patch releases corrected PBR, WebGL, event-loop, DICOM, serialization, filtering, and platform issues.

The 2026 Kitware articles translated this broad engineering program into user-facing themes. VTK 9.6.0, VTK the Polyglot, and the Tcl/Tk article addressed releases and language access. The trame/Jupyter and Python-pipeline posts focused on interactive workflows. The VTK.js v35 article described the complementary JavaScript-native web toolkit. Two WebGPU articles explained the case for one portable graphics layer and its practical limitations. The modern-workflows overview connected web, AI, simulation, and notebook use. The polyhedron and vertex-pulling posts documented measured performance work and opportunities to reduce slow paths and duplicate mappers.

On May 13, 2026, the VTK developer community held a maintenance-oriented hackathon. The event closed more than 50 issues, applied more than 40 labels, assigned more than 25 issues, and opened more than 15 merge requests. Work included thread-safety and data-race investigation, crashes in XML/polyhedron/molecular readers, WASM and Apple build improvements, Exodus/XDMF/XML corrections, C++ toolchain work, rendering fixes, and ownership of longer-term concurrency tasks.

VTK Days was discussed from January through June as a distinct, more expansive community event. The proposed goals were to recognize internal and external contributions, present major work such as WebGPU, WASM, serialization, widgets, documentation, and simplification, invite new contributors, and retain a hands-on coding component. Planning favored multiple shorter sessions or days to accommodate Europe and the U.S. West Coast, potentially separating presentations from programming. August or September was discussed as a tentative window, and organizers emphasized a four-month announcement lead time and repeated promotion. The May hackathon's 15 participants, including six external contributors, supplied evidence of community interest; some external contributors expressed willingness to attend a future gathering in Clifton Park. No final public VTK Days date or completed event was found by August 3.

VTK 9.7 moved through three release candidates from late June through July 28. Its final release was scheduled for August 3, with WebGPU available for opt-in testing before the proposed VTK 10 default-backend change. When this report was checked on August 3, the official PyPI page still listed VTK 9.6.2 as the latest stable package; the final 9.7.0 publication was therefore not yet independently confirmed.

The main conclusion is that the releases, articles, hackathon, and VTK Days planning should be understood as one program. Releases create stable technical baselines, articles explain their significance, focused maintenance improves reliability, and VTK Days would provide a recurring space for recognition, presentations, recruitment, and collaborative work. The pattern strengthens adoption, contributor participation, and the project's ability to carry major transitions without abandoning compatibility and evidence-based engineering.
